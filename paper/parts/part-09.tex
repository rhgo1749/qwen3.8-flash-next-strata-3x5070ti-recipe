Table~\ref{tab:workloads} separates no-reuse prompt processing from warm decode. For matched $\sim$15K prompts, fiction, coding, and reasoning PP all fall between 2542 and 2554 tok/s, within roughly 0.5\% of one another. Short $\sim$1.5K prompts are slower in tok/s because fixed costs dominate. A 109,958-token long review reaches 2554.09 tok/s PP and 43.32~s TTFT.

Decode behaves differently. Fiction has the highest expert-cache hit rates (89--91\%) but is slower than coding, whose cache hit rate is about 86--87\%. Coding also has higher speculative acceptance (74--75\% versus 51--56\% for fiction). Figure~\ref{fig:workloadscatter} therefore shows only that cache-hit rate alone does not determine the observed TG ordering. Because context length and workload class are not jointly controlled---especially for the long-review point---we do not infer that speculative acceptance is the cause of the difference.
